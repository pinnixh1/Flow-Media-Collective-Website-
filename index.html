Behind the scenes · Fast Lane Drive Austin × Marché ATX

Six Courses Above Austin

An invitation-only dinner, a skyline view and a 100-year-old cognac. Here's how we covered a night designed to be savored.

In May 2025, Fast Lane Drive Austin invited its members to a private dinner overlooking the Austin skyline. Marché ATX curated the evening. Their idea was the French countryside brought into the city, with candlelight, floral linens and a menu built from organic, locally sourced ingredients.

We were there to capture it. That meant two jobs at once: show the people who weren't in the room what they missed, and give the people who were there something worth keeping.

The details did the talking

Guests arrived in cocktail attire and found miniature sports cars as place cards, with conversation cards at every setting to get strangers talking. A classical guitarist played while the room filled. Nothing was left to chance except who you'd be seated next to.

Those details became our shot list. We photographed the place cards, the glassware and the plates as carefully as the guests, because in a room like that the small things tell you how much care went into the night.

The menu

Marché ATX served six courses:

  • Beet tartare with citrus and capers
  • Black cod crudo with strawberries and charred allium vinaigrette
  • Spring pea and goat cheese ravioli with fennel brown butter
  • Green gazpacho granitée with cucumber, green apple and herbs
  • Grilled venison loin with roasted sweet potatoes and bordelaise
  • Chocolate hazelnut mousse with cognac, raspberry and brittle

To close the night, guests were served 100-year-old Louis XIII cognac in crystal glasses that rang in a perfect C when raised for a toast. We made sure we had that moment.

What we made

The photographs went out as two carousel posts, 25 images in all. We also cut a 17-second film that opens on a single line: you step into an invitation-only evening where horsepower meets high society in Austin, Texas. It's short on purpose. A film about a dinner should leave you hungry.

Here's to the rooms where everyone belongs, but only a few are invited.From the post that closed out the night

Process · Fast Lane Drive × Red Box Restoration

Red Drive: 14 Reels in Three Weeks

One Saturday, one venue, four hours. This is the campaign we built to get Austin there, and what we posted after.

On Saturday, March 29, 2025, Fast Lane Drive Texas and Red Box Restoration opened the doors for a supercar meetup and after party, from 11 AM to 3 PM, and open to the public. Fast Lane Drive is a members' club, so this was a chance to show the wider city what it's about. Our job was to make people want to be there.

We posted 14 Reels between March 18 and April 5. Looking back, they fall into five stages.

How it ran

  1. March 18–19 · AnnounceThe first Reels set the scene: the cars, the venue, the date, and a simple message that tickets were limited.
  2. March 24–27 · Introduce the peopleWe put faces on the club. That meant interviews with Brad Ballard, President of Fast Lane Drive Texas, about why the club exists, a birthday shout-out, and a McLaren 765LT spotted at Red Box to keep the cars in the feed.
  3. March 28 · Count down"Two days to go" and "one day to go," posted the same afternoon.
  4. March 29 · Show upThe day itself: supercars, live entertainment, food and the after party at Red Box.
  5. March 31–April 5 · Say thank youA full walkthrough of the day, sponsor thank-yous for Torchy's Tacos, Chisos Boots and Elite Residential Group, and footage of the drive in.

Sell the cars, then the people

Supercars stop the scroll. That's why the first Reels led with them. But people don't buy a ticket to look at a car they could see in a photo. They come for the people, so the middle of the campaign belonged to the members, in the driver's seat and in their own words.

The line we kept coming back to was the one the club uses itself: come for the cars, stay for the connections.

The thank-you is part of the campaign

The sponsor recaps weren't an afterthought. They thanked the partners publicly, they showed everyone who missed the day what it looked like, and they set up the next event before this one had cooled down.

Huge thanks to everyone who joined us for an amazing event with @fastlanedrive! We're incredibly grateful for the opportunity to host. Special shoutout to @flowmediacollective for bringing it all together.Red Box Restoration, after the event

Perspective · Fast Lane Drive

It's Not About the Cars

We started Flow Media Collective filming a supercar club. The most important thing we learned is that the cars aren't the story.

We introduced Flow Media Collective in January 2025 with a Reel made alongside Fast Lane Drive Texas, filmed on the club's first drive of the year. It's easy to film a supercar well. The light hits the paint, the engine does the sound design, and the shot looks expensive. We learned quickly that a beautiful car isn't a story.

The club says it better than we can: it's more than the cars. It's the community you arrive with, and the one you stay for. That idea has shaped how we've filmed everything since.

Start with a person

Our member stories begin with someone, not something. One of the first was Grant's, who grew up around airports and traded jet fuel for supercars. The cars appear in the film, but the reason to watch is him. Many of our interviews happen in the car, on the drive, where people tend to forget the camera is there.

Let the city in

When the club asked for a film to introduce its Austin chapter, we didn't stay in the parking lot. We took the cars downtown, put the skyline and Lady Bird Lake in frame, and went overhead to show the cars moving through the city. Austin is part of what the members are signing up for, so it belonged in the film.

Follow the whole day

For a day at The W, we covered everything that happened, not just the arrival. That meant a morning drive through Austin's backroads, valet at the hotel, a private lunch with a view and an afternoon by the pool. The one-minute film moves through all of it. The cars get you there, and the rest of the day is why people go.

Not just a car club; a way of life.Fast Lane Drive Austin
